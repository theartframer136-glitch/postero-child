Pichwai art is the painting tradition of Nathdwara, a temple town near Udaipur in Rajasthan. For more than three centuries its painters have made large cloth paintings to hang behind the image of Shrinathji, the child form of Lord Krishna. Today a Pichwai is just as likely to hang in a living room in New Jersey or Texas as in a temple. Here is what the paintings mean, how to read their symbols, and how to choose and hang a large Pichwai in an American home.

What does Pichwai mean?

The name comes from two Hindi words: pichh, back, and wai, hanging. A Pichwai is literally "the hanging at the back". In the Shrinathji temple the painted cloth is the backdrop to the deity, and it is changed with the seasons and festivals, so the shrine looks different through the year. The tradition belongs to the Pushtimarg, the devotional path of Krishna worship founded by Vallabhacharya in the 16th century, in which Krishna is loved and served like a cherished child.

Who is Shrinathji?

Shrinathji is Krishna at about seven years old, lifting Govardhan hill with his raised left hand to shelter the people and cows of Braj from Indra's storm. In Pichwai paintings he is shown dark-skinned, with large lotus eyes, richly dressed and garlanded, often standing at the centre with priests, gopis and cows around him. The image stands for protection: the god who holds up a mountain to keep his devotees safe.

How to read a Pichwai: the symbols

  • Lotus: purity and beauty. Lotus-pond Pichwais, called kamal talai, were hung in the summer months for their cool, calm feeling.
  • Cows: Krishna as Gopala, the cowherd. Cows stand for abundance and nourishment, and paintings of the cows alone are a Pichwai theme of their own.
  • Gopis: the cowherd women of Braj, representing pure devotion to Krishna.
  • Pearl garlands and fine jewellery: the shringar, the daily dressing of the deity, painted in loving detail as an act of service.
  • Peacocks, banana trees and elephants: the gardens of Braj and royal festivity.
  • The full moon and the circle dance: Sharad Purnima and the raas leela, Krishna dancing with the gopis.

The festival Pichwais

Because the temple changes its backdrop through the year, many Pichwais belong to a season or festival: Janmashtami for Krishna's birth, Sharad Purnima for the autumn full moon, Annakut and Govardhan Puja after Diwali with their mountains of food offerings, Gopashtami for the cows, and Holi with its spring colours. You do not need to change your painting with the calendar at home, but knowing the occasion behind a design helps you choose one that means something to your family.

Traditional and modern Pichwai

Traditional Pichwais were painted on cotton with natural pigments and real gold, by families of painters who passed the craft down through generations, and they can take months to finish. Modern Pichwai keeps the motifs (the lotus, the cows, Shrinathji's face) but often isolates one element on a calm background, in softer colours that suit contemporary interiors. Both have a place: a detailed traditional composition makes a statement in a formal living room, while a single lotus or cow design sits well in a minimal apartment.

Choosing a large Pichwai for your wall

A Pichwai is made to be seen at a distance, so it rewards a generous size. As a rule, the art should fill about two thirds of the width of the wall space or furniture it hangs above.

Where to hang a Pichwai

  • The pooja room or mandir wall: the most traditional place, echoing the temple. Our pooja room wall ideas show how to set one up even in a closet or corner.
  • The living room focal wall: a large Pichwai is a conversation piece, and the one wall guests remember.
  • The dining area: cow Pichwais, with their theme of abundance and nourishment, are a natural fit.
  • Height: hang it with the centre of the canvas about 57 to 60 inches from the floor, or 8 to 10 inches above the top of the sofa or console.
  • Avoid: direct afternoon sun, the wall behind a stove, and damp rooms such as bathrooms.

Frequently asked questions

What is Pichwai art?

Pichwai art is a style of devotional painting from Nathdwara in Rajasthan, made to hang behind the image of Shrinathji, the child form of Krishna. The paintings are known for lotuses, cows, gopis, festival scenes, rich colour and very fine detail.

Is it auspicious to keep a Pichwai painting at home?

Yes. Pichwai paintings show Krishna as Shrinathji, the protector who lifted Govardhan hill, together with symbols of purity and abundance such as the lotus and the cow. Many families hang one in the pooja room or living room as a blessing for the home.

What is the difference between Pichwai and Tanjore painting?

Pichwai comes from Rajasthan and is painted flat on cloth, centred on Shrinathji and Krishna's life in Braj. Tanjore painting comes from Tamil Nadu, is made on wooden board with raised gesso work and gold foil, and shows many deities.

Which Pichwai is best for a living room?

A large 3×4 ft design with a strong centre, such as Shrinathji with lotuses or with pearl garlands, makes the best focal point above a sofa. For a long, low wall, choose a landscape design such as Krishna with elephants.
