A pooja room in an American apartment is rarely a room. It is a closet, a corner of the living room, a nook under the stairs or the top of a bookshelf. The wall behind the mandir is what turns that corner into a sacred space, and canvas art is the easiest way to do it without drilling into a rental's walls or building a wooden temple you cannot take to the next home.

Start with the direction

Vastu places the pooja space in the north-east corner of the home when possible, with the deities facing west or east so you face east or west while praying. In an apartment you work with what you have: a north or east wall is good, and any quiet corner away from the bathroom and the bed is acceptable. What matters more than the compass is that the space is clean, lit and used every day.

Idea 1: one large deity canvas as the backdrop

The simplest and most striking approach. A single 2×3 ft or 3×2 ft canvas of your family's chosen deity hung above a narrow console or a floating shelf becomes the mandir wall. The shelf holds the lamp, the incense and the small idols; the canvas gives the space its presence. Radha Krishna, Lakshmi Ganesh and Tirupati Balaji are the most chosen for this role.

Idea 2: the closet mandir

Many families convert a hall closet. Remove the door or leave it open, paint the inside a warm colour, add an LED strip under each shelf, and hang a canvas on the back wall. A vertical 2×3 ft portrait format fits most closet depths. Keep the lowest shelf at a height you can reach while seated on the floor.

Idea 3: a gallery of deities

For a wider wall, three smaller canvases in a row, for example Ganesha in the centre with Lakshmi and Saraswati either side, or Shiva, Vishnu and Devi. Keep the same frame finish and the same height so the group reads as one. Our Hindu Gods collection covers the full pantheon in matching floating frames.

Idea 4: a rental-friendly corner

No drilling allowed? A tall floating-frame canvas can lean on a low cabinet against the wall, with the idols in front. Command strips rated for the canvas weight hold a 2×3 ft piece safely on painted drywall. Add a small rug to mark the floor space and a battery lamp if there is no outlet.

Lighting, colour and what to avoid

  • Warm white light, never cool blue. A small picture light above the canvas is ideal.
  • Walls in cream, pale yellow, light saffron or soft blue. Avoid black and dark grey behind the deities.
  • No mirrors facing the mandir, no pooja space under a staircase or sharing a wall with a toilet if it can be helped.
  • Deity images should be complete and serene. Avoid images of deities in anger or battle in the pooja space.

Why canvas rather than a framed print or poster

Incense, lamps and oil are hard on paper. A canvas with archival inks and a floating frame has no glass to smoke up and no paper to yellow, and it wipes clean with a dry cloth. It is also light enough for an apartment wall and travels well when you move.

Frequently asked questions

Which deity should go in the centre of the pooja wall?

Your family's ishta devata, the deity your household worships most. Where there is no single one, Ganesha in the centre is the traditional choice, since every pooja begins with him.

Can the pooja corner be in the bedroom?

Tradition prefers not, but in a studio or one-bedroom apartment it is common. Keep it away from the foot of the bed and cover or close it at night.

Can I hang a deity canvas with adhesive strips?

Yes, for a 2×3 ft floating-frame canvas, use strips rated for at least 16 lb in total and press each strip for 30 seconds on a clean wall.
