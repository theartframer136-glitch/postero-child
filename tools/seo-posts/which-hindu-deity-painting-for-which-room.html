Choosing a deity painting for the home is partly devotion and partly placement. Each deity carries a meaning, and tradition matches that meaning to a room: Ganesha at the door, Lakshmi where wealth is counted, Krishna where the family gathers. Here is a room-by-room guide for Indian homes in the USA.

Entrance and foyer: Ganesha

Lord Ganesha removes obstacles and blesses beginnings, so he greets everyone who enters. Hang a Ganesha canvas on the wall inside the front door or facing it, at eye level. A 2×3 ft piece suits most US foyers; a 2.5×3 ft piece works for a double-height entry.

Living room: Radha Krishna, Ram Darbar, Seven Horses

The living room is where the family sits together, so the art should speak of harmony. Radha Krishna is the classic choice, divine love and togetherness in rich colour above the sofa. Ram Darbar, Lord Ram with Sita, Lakshman and Hanuman, is read as the ideal family and righteous living. For a home that wants a Vastu statement rather than a deity, the seven running horses on the south or east wall is the traditional pick.

Pooja room: your ishta devata

The mandir wall belongs to the deity your family worships most. Common choices: Tirupati Balaji in Telugu and Tamil homes, Krishna or Swaminarayan in Gujarati homes, Durga Maa in Bengali homes, Lord Shiva in many North Indian families. One large, serene image is better than several small ones. See our full Hindu Gods collection.

Home office and study: Saraswati, Shiva, Hanuman

Saraswati, the goddess of knowledge, suits a study or a child's homework corner. A meditating Shiva calms a home office and is a favourite of people who work under pressure. Hanuman brings strength and discipline and is often chosen for a gym corner or a student's room. The seven horses also belong here, on the wall you face while working.

Dining room and kitchen: Annapurna, Krishna with cows

Annapurna, the goddess of nourishment, is traditional for the dining area. Krishna among cows, or a Pichwai of Shrinathji, brings the same warmth with a decorative, festive feel that suits a dining wall.

Bedroom: Radha Krishna, calm images only

Tradition keeps the bedroom gentle. A Radha Krishna in soft tones is the one deity image widely used there, hung on a side wall rather than above the bed. Avoid fierce forms, battle scenes and the seven horses in the bedroom.

Where not to hang a deity image

  • Never on a wall shared with a bathroom, and never in the bathroom itself.
  • Not on the staircase wall where people walk above it.
  • Not facing a mirror, and not directly opposite the front door where it is behind you as you leave.
  • Not below eye level, and never on the floor.

Frequently asked questions

Can I hang more than one deity in the same room?

Yes. Keep them at the same height and in the same frame finish, and give the family deity the central or largest position.

Which deity painting is right for a new home?

Ganesha at the entrance and Lakshmi Ganesh in the pooja space are the traditional first two pieces for a Griha Pravesh.

Is a Buddha painting considered a deity image?

In most Hindu homes the Buddha is placed as a symbol of peace rather than worshipped, so it goes in the living room, bedroom or a meditation corner rather than the mandir.
