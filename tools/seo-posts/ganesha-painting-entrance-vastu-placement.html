A Ganesha painting at the entrance is the first thing most Indian families hang in a new American home, and for good reason: Lord Ganesha is Vighnaharta, the remover of obstacles, and the entrance is where everything enters the house. Here is how Vastu places him, which form to choose, and what size suits a US foyer.

Where exactly at the entrance

Vastu gives three good options:

  • Inside, facing the door: on the wall you see as you step in. Ganesha greets everyone who arrives. This is the most common placement in US homes with a proper foyer.
  • Inside, on the wall beside the door: at eye level, so he is seen on the way in and the way out.
  • Above the door frame, inside: a small horizontal piece, traditional in India, less common in American homes with tall doors.

Avoid placing the image outside the door exposed to weather, and avoid a placement where Ganesha's back faces the main house. One traditional rule says that if an image faces outward above the door, a second image should face inward, so the home is not left with his back turned.

Direction

The entrance wall facing north, east or north-east is best. If your front door opens south, place the painting on the inside wall so that Ganesha faces north or east, rather than on the south wall itself.

Which form of Ganesha

  • Seated, calm, trunk curving left: the household form, for peace and prosperity at home. The left-curving trunk (vamamukhi) is the one tradition prefers indoors.
  • Standing or dancing Ganesha: energetic, better for offices and studios than the home entrance.
  • With Lakshmi: the pair, wisdom and wealth together, is the classic Diwali and housewarming image and suits a foyer that leads into the living room.
  • Modern or abstract Ganesha: perfectly acceptable, as long as the form is complete and serene. Many Indian-American homes mix a contemporary Ganesha with modern furniture.

Choose an image with a modak in his hand, a symbol of the sweetness of reward, and a mouse at his feet, his vehicle. Avoid images where any part of the deity is cut off by the edge of the canvas.

Hang the centre of the canvas 57 to 60 inches from the floor. If there is a console below, leave 6 to 8 inches between the table top and the frame.

What goes with it

A small brass diya or a battery tea light on the console, a toran over the door for festivals, and a warm picture light above the canvas. Keep shoes and the shoe rack away from the wall below the painting.

Frequently asked questions

Can a Ganesha painting face the front door?

Yes, from the inside. Ganesha facing the door from the wall opposite it is the most common placement; it is Ganesha placed outside the door, facing away from the house, that tradition cautions against.

Is one Ganesha image enough for the home?

Yes. One at the entrance, and the family's main deity in the pooja space, is the usual arrangement. More images are fine as long as each is placed respectfully.

Which colour Ganesha is best for the entrance?

Warm colours, saffron, red, gold and cream, are traditional for the entrance. White Ganesha is associated with peace and suits a pooja room.
